# Three taps beat every LinkedIn feed

Nothing official will push your LinkedIn posts into aiuxaleem.com, so the least manual route that stays inside LinkedIn's rules and your own is to capture each post yourself at the moment you publish it: **about three taps on the phone's share sheet (or one click of a personal browser extension on the desktop), then one click in the admin, for $0 a month**. The read door is shut from every side: `r_member_social`, the only permission that lists a member's own posts, is "closed" to new applicants; the Community Management product admits only registered legal organisations; the EU Data Portability API refuses anyone outside the EEA and Switzerland; LinkedIn has had no RSS since 19 December 2013; and Zapier, IFTTT, Make, Pipedream and n8n have no "new post by me" trigger. Zero-tap automation therefore exists only as scraping, which at thirty posts a month costs $0 to $1.50 on Apify or Bright Data but places your data source outside LinkedIn's User Agreement, the ground on which LinkedIn closed Proxycurl, enjoined hiQ and ended Shield. The reverse route (write in your admin, push to LinkedIn through the self-serve `w_member_social` scope) is official and gives the site the text, but it moves your writing desk out of the LinkedIn app, needs a LinkedIn Page to anchor the developer app, stops every 60 days until you re-consent, and cannot author a LinkedIn Article. Your admin already contains nine-tenths of the capture route: a key-protected inbound API (`POST /api/resources`), a drafts table, a feed-to-drafts import for YouTube, a resolver for `lnkd.in` links and a click-to-load embed card; what is missing is one sibling endpoint, a share target for the phone, and a decision from you. What capture cannot do is catch posts you forget to share, store the post's body and images (the site keeps only a title, a link and a date), or back-fill older posts, for which the manual "Download your data" archive is the single official source.

## Every official read route is shut to an individual in India

The one LinkedIn API that lists a member's own posts, `GET /rest/posts?author=urn:li:person:…`, requires the `r_member_social` permission, and LinkedIn's own FAQ states that **"`r_member_social` is a closed permission. We're not accepting access requests at this time due to resource constraints"** ([Community Management Overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/community-management-overview)). The same wording has stood since at least 2023, so it is a position, not a pause. The product that contains it is shut a second time: Community Management APIs are **"only available to registered legal organizations for commercial use cases only"**, applicants need a registered address, a business-domain email ("Personal email addresses won't pass the vetting process") and a Company Page super admin to vouch for the app ([Community Management App Review](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management-app-review)), and even an organisation that got in would read that "displaying a company page feed on your website or any other digital channel off LinkedIn is not an Approved use case" ([Marketing API FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/lms-faq)). The two products anyone can add without review grant exactly `openid`, `profile`, `email` and the write-only `w_member_social` ([Getting Access to LinkedIn APIs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/authentication/getting-access)); none of them reads a post.

The European escape hatch does not reach Hyderabad. LinkedIn's Member Data Portability API, built for the Digital Markets Act, does return a member's posts and articles, but "this feature is available only for LinkedIn members located in the European Economic Area and Switzerland, so only those members are able to consent and generate an access token" ([Member Data Portability (Member)](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/dma/member-data-portability/member-data-portability-member/)). The consent screen itself is the gate; there is no form to apply through, and changing a profile location to pass it would misrepresent you. Nor is there a feed to subscribe to: LinkedIn retired its last RSS feed on **19 December 2013** ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/linkedin-will-kill-rss-support-december-19)), the oEmbed provider registry has no LinkedIn entry and a guessed `linkedin.com/oembed` endpoint answers 404 ([oembed.com/providers.json](https://oembed.com/providers.json)), and a newsletter "subscribe" means push, in-app and email notifications only ([LinkedIn Help: Newsletters](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/111414)). The automation platforms confirm the vacuum from the other side: Zapier's LinkedIn app has **zero triggers** ([Zapier help](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8495987891853-How-to-get-started-with-LinkedIn-on-Zapier)), IFTTT lists "Triggers (0)" ([IFTTT](https://ifttt.com/linkedin)), Make's one trigger is "Watch Company Posts" ([Make](https://www.make.com/en/integrations/linkedin)), Pipedream's is "New Organization Post Created" ([Pipedream](https://pipedream.com/apps/linkedin)) and n8n's node only creates ([n8n](https://docs.n8n.io/integrations/builtin/app-nodes/n8n-nodes-base.linkedin/)). The 2025 to 2026 change log adds member analytics for approved partners (post statistics in June 2025, Boost in October 2026) and nothing that reopens reading for individuals ([Recent Changes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/integrations/recent-changes)).

What LinkedIn does give an individual is a manual archive and a per-post embed. "Download your data" is desktop-only, splits into a "specific categories" tier headed "Available within 10 minutes" and a "larger download" headed "Available within 48 hours", keeps the link alive for 72 hours and offers no schedule or API; Articles sit in the fast tier, but Shares, the file that holds your posts, sits in the slow one ([LinkedIn Help: Download your data](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339364)). Practitioners report that `Shares.csv` carries date, link, text and visibility but no engagement counts and omits polls and document posts ([Trevor Fox](https://trevorfox.com/2026/07/how-to-export-linkedin-posts/)); LinkedIn's Help says the Articles category "Contains URLs", practitioners say the folder holds HTML copies ([Espirian](https://espirian.co.uk/linkedin-data-archive/)), so read your own download before relying on either. The embed is the official display route your site already uses: "Only posts that an author has shared as Public can be embedded", it is generated one post at a time on desktop, and "We don't offer the option to embed multi-photo posts or reposts with commentary"; if you delete the post, "the embeds will also get deleted" ([LinkedIn Help a529065](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a529065)).

Two measurements taken on 3 November 2026 matter for your rules. First, the embed endpoint answered a bare request with `frame-ancestors *` and `set-cookie` headers for **`bcookie` and `bscookie` expiring 3 November 2027** plus a one-day `lidc`, with no login involved ([embed endpoint](https://www.linkedin.com/embed/feed/update/urn:li:share:7382521851400372224)), so the iframe plants LinkedIn's cookies the moment it loads, and the site's click-to-load facade is what keeps a page cookie-free until a visitor opts in. Second, `urn:li:share:7382521851400372224` returned 200 while the same digits as `urn:li:activity:` returned 404: activity, share and ugcPost numbers are different identifiers for the same post and are not interchangeable ([feedsweep probe](https://github.com/macieklamberski/feedsweep/pull/1298)). Your site derives `urn:li:activity:<n>` from the "Copy link to post" URL (`linkedinUrn` in [src/lib/content-schemas.ts](../src/lib/content-schemas.ts) lines 134-140) and its behaviour tests block every LinkedIn host ([scripts/quality/check-blog.mjs](../scripts/quality/check-blog.mjs) line 7), so whether those activity-form embeds actually load has never been checked by the tooling; the research measured only a share URN loading. One press of "Load the post here" on `/posts` settles it. Fetching the public post page server-side to learn its title is not a sanctioned substitute: LinkedIn answers automated clients with the non-standard HTTP 999, most readily from data-centre and cloud ranges ([http.dev](https://http.dev/999); [ScrapingDog](https://www.scrapingdog.com/webscraping-problems/999-response-when-scraping-linkedin-profile/)), which is exactly where a Vercel function runs.

## Zero taps exist only as scraping, at $0 to $1.50 a month

If you accept a crawler, the price is nearly nothing and the delivery is automatic. Apify's cookie-free actors take a profile URL and return each post's text, date, URL and URN, images, document carousels and reaction counts. harvestapi is listed "from $1.50 / 1,000 posts" in its header and "$2 per 1k posts" in its README, offers a `postedLimit: '24h'` input that makes a nightly "only what is new" run possible, and shows "100.0% runs succeeded" across 46,247 users ([Apify: harvestapi](https://apify.com/harvestapi/linkedin-profile-posts)); apimaestro is "from $5.00 / 1,000 results", exposes `full_urn` (`urn:li:ugcPost:…`) and document `page_count`, and shows "98.2% runs succeeded" ([Apify: apimaestro](https://apify.com/apimaestro/linkedin-profile-posts)). At thirty posts a month that is **$0.05 to $1.50**, inside the **$5 monthly credit of Apify's Free plan** ([Apify pricing](https://apify.com/pricing)); schedules run on cron and a webhook fires on `ACTOR.RUN.SUCCEEDED` with the run id, after which the dataset is one JSON call away ([Apify webhook events](https://docs.apify.com/platform/integrations/webhooks/events)). Bright Data's LinkedIn Posts scraper is "pay per successful record" at $1.50 per 1,000 with **5,000 free records a month** and delivers by webhook, S3 or API ([Bright Data pricing](https://brightdata.com/pricing/web-scraper); [Bright Data docs](https://docs.brightdata.com/products/scrapers/linkedin/introduction.md)). Cheap is not durable: one March 2026 account of a self-built LinkedIn scraper has it running "about two months before a LinkedIn detection update broke it, and then breaking again after about three weeks" ([cotera.co](https://cotera.co/articles/linkedin-scraping-tools-comparison.md)), and a "runs succeeded" figure records that a run finished, not that its data was complete.

The tools that run as *you* cost more and expose your account. Unipile reads "List all User's Posts" for `me` through your own LinkedIn session for a **$55 a month minimum** ([Unipile pricing](https://www.unipile.com/pricing/)); Phantombuster's Activity Extractor needs your `li_at` cookie and costs about **$69 a month** on third-party price pages, and a cookie that expires mid-run stops it ([La Growth Machine](https://lagrowthmachine.com/phantombuster-pricing/); [Phantombuster support](https://support.phantombuster.com/hc/en-us/articles/26971008280850-How-to-use-the-LinkedIn-Activity-Extractor)); Apify's one cookie-based actor is where the developer warns that running "multiple instances of the actor simultaneously" means "your account will get suspended" and a user reports exactly that ([curious_coder issue](https://beta-securitybyobscurity.apify.com/curious_coder/linkedin-post-search-scraper/issues/prevent-being-suspen-7hzTqfPz9J2B6ec0n)). AuthoredUp is the one member-side tool with a documented JSON API for your own posts, including `activity_urn` since 30 December 2025, at 100 requests an hour on any licence ([AuthoredUp API](https://help.authoredup.com/api-docs/overview)), on an Individual plan of **$19.95 a month** ([AuthoredUp pricing](https://authoredup.com/pricing)); it collects through a Chrome extension in your session, the same category as Shield, which is winding down in 2026 because "Both Google and LinkedIn made it clear that we could not continue operating Shield as it was built" ([Taplio quoting shieldapp.ai](https://taplio.com/blog/shield-alternatives)). The RSS generators do not reach a personal profile: rss.app's LinkedIn page and its January 2026 help article name company pages, company newsletters and job searches, never `/in/` profiles ([rss.app help](https://help.rss.app/en/articles/10656832-how-to-create-rss-feeds-from-linkedin)), and RSS-Bridge's public bridge list has no LinkedIn bridge ([RSS-Bridge](https://rss-bridge.org/bridge01/)). Proxycurl, once the best-known profile API, shut down on **4 July 2025** "to comply with the legal settlement with LinkedIn" ([Nubela](https://nubela.co/blog/goodbye-proxycurl/)).

| Route | What it returns | Delivery | Price at about 30 posts a month | Whose account is exposed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apify harvestapi (no cookies) | text, date, URL, images, document covers and page count, engagement; `postedLimit: '24h'` | cron schedule, webhook, dataset JSON | $0.05 to $0.60, inside the $5 free credit | nobody's: the vendor crawls |
| Apify apimaestro (no cookies) | text, `full_urn`, images, document `page_count`, article, stats | same | $0.15 to $1.50, inside the free credit | nobody's |

| Unipile | own posts as `me`, cursor paged | REST pull | $55 a month minimum | yours: runs on your login |
| Phantombuster Activity Extractor | recent posts, comments, reactions | Phantom schedule | about $69 a month | yours: runs on your `li_at` cookie |
| rss.app, RSS-Bridge, Zapier, IFTTT, Make, n8n, Pipedream | no personal-profile post source | none | none | none |

The legal shape is consistent across all of this. You own what you post: the User Agreement effective 3 November 2025 says "You own all of your original content that you provide to us, but you also grant us a non-exclusive license to it", and "you may choose to make it available to others" ([User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)), so republishing your own posts on your own site needs no one's permission. What the agreement bans is the method: section 8.2 forbids using "software, devices, scripts, robots or any other means or processes" to scrape the Services and "bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services", and the prohibited-software page extends that to "crawlers, bots, browser plug-ins, and browser extensions", warning that members who use them "risk having their accounts restricted or shut down" ([LinkedIn Help a1341387](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)). Enforcement has followed the method, not the content: hiQ ended in a December 2022 consent judgment barring scraping "whether or not logged in" with a $500,000 judgment ([Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/)), Proxycurl settled and closed, and in March 2026 LinkedIn's newsroom named "a browser extension, script, or third party tool" as the thing it acts against for automated comments ([LinkedIn newsroom](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/authentic-content-and-conversations)). A cookie-free crawler keeps your account out of the path and puts the breach, and the continuity risk, on the vendor; a tool on your session puts your account in it. Neither is inside the agreement, which is why this report treats scraping as a choice only you can make rather than a default.

## Publishing from the site is official, but it moves your writing desk

LinkedIn's write door is open to individuals, and it is the one official way a site can "know" a member's post: the site creates it. The "Share on LinkedIn" product grants `w_member_social` through the Developer Portal with no review ([Share on LinkedIn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/consumer/integrations/self-serve/share-on-linkedin)), with one catch for a person without a company: "For certain functionality and products, you're required to associate your app with a LinkedIn Page", whose super admin "has 30 days to approve the request" ([Associate an app with a LinkedIn Page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a548360)), so you would create a Page for yourself first; whether a one-person Page is acceptable is not stated anywhere. The member id comes from the OpenID Connect `userinfo` endpoint ([Sign In with LinkedIn using OpenID Connect](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/consumer/integrations/self-serve/sign-in-with-linkedin-v2)) and posts go to `POST https://api.linkedin.com/rest/posts` with a `Linkedin-Version: YYYYMM` header; the post's URN comes back in the `x-restli-id` header, which is exactly the identifier the embed card needs ([Posts API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/posts-api)). The API covers text, single images, **2 to 20 images**, MP4 video from three seconds to 30 minutes, PDF and Office documents up to 100 MB and 300 pages, and polls of 2 to 4 options; organic carousels are "currently not supported" ([Posts API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/posts-api); [Documents API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/documents-api); [Videos API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/videos-api)). Posts hold up to **3,000 characters** ([LinkedIn Help a528176](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528176)) and the member limit is **150 requests a day** ([Share on LinkedIn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/consumer/integrations/self-serve/share-on-linkedin)).

Three limits shape the daily experience. There is no scheduling: for `lifecycleState`, "PUBLISHED is the only accepted field during creation" ([Post Schema](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/post-api-schema)), so a queue would have to live in your admin and fire from a Vercel cron, which on the Hobby plan runs once a day with ±59 minutes of jitter ([Vercel Cron](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/usage-and-pricing)). A link post is not previewed for you: "The Posts API does not support URL scraping for article post creation", so the admin must upload the thumbnail through the Images API and supply title and description ([Posts API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/posts-api)); your build already draws share images, so this is work, not a wall. And the API's "article" content type is that link post, not a LinkedIn Article: nothing among the documented organic types (Text, Images, Videos, Documents, Article, MultiImage, Poll) authors a Pulse article or a newsletter issue, so the long-form half of your writing stays in LinkedIn's own editor whatever you decide. The token is the recurring chore: "all access tokens are issued with a 60-day lifespan" and "LinkedIn does not generate long-lived access tokens"; programmatic refresh tokens exist "for all approved Marketing Developer Platform (MDP) partners" and nobody else ([3-Legged OAuth](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/authentication/authorization-code-flow); [Refresh Tokens](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/authentication/programmatic-refresh-tokens)). The saving grace is that re-authorising before day 60, while still logged in to LinkedIn, skips the consent screen and is one redirect; the admin would show a "Reconnect LinkedIn" state from about day 50 and refuse to publish, saying why, when the token is near its end. The `Linkedin-Version` must also move at least yearly: 202511 is sunset on 16 November 2026 ([Versioning](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/versioning)), so the version belongs in a setting and a 426 must fail loudly.

On reach, the honest position is that LinkedIn has never said. Every enforcement case found concerns browser-session or scraping access, not the official write API; no study from 2024 to 2026 isolates API-posted from composer-posted reach, and the vendor blogs asserting "no penalty" sell schedulers. What is documented is unrelated to method and will hit any post that links back to your site: the 2026 Algorithm Insights report puts the median reach reduction for posts with external links at **18.8 %** ([Vivian Voss citing van der Blom](https://vivianvoss.net/blog/linkedin-reach-you-did-not-earn)), against a general decline of about 47 % in views in the 2025 report ([SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm)). No post-level "via <app>" label was found; the only attribution LinkedIn has built is the 2026 "connected apps" section on profiles, open to about twenty partner tools such as Buffer, not to your own app ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/linkedin-links-app-usage-to-profiles-for-over-1-million-members/)). If you want the token chore to be someone else's, hosted partners hold the connection on a yearly cycle: Zernio (formerly Late) gives the **first two accounts free with the full API** ([Zernio pricing](https://zernio.com/pricing)), Publora is free for 15 posts a month and **$5.99 an account** beyond ([Publora pricing](https://publora.com/pricing.md)), Buffer's free tier includes an API key but its legacy REST API is retired on **1 February 2027** in favour of GraphQL ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/legacy-rest-api-retired/)), and Ayrshare at $149 a month is priced for products, not people ([Ayrshare pricing](https://www.ayrshare.com/pricing)). Self-hosting Postiz or Mixpost does not escape the token: both need your own LinkedIn app, and Postiz's docs say that without the partner-only Advertising API "you cannot refresh tokens" ([Postiz LinkedIn provider](https://docs.postiz.com/providers/linkedin)). The IndieWeb offers no shortcut: Bridgy publishes to Bluesky, Flickr, GitHub and Mastodon and not to LinkedIn ([brid.gy/about](https://brid.gy/about)); the one maintained open-source piece is a GitHub Action that posts your RSS feed's newest item with a 60-day token kept as a secret ([Linkedin-RSS action](https://github.com/marketplace/actions/linkedin-rss)), which inherits the same expiry.

The reverse route is therefore the route for a different owner: one who wants the site to be the canonical home of the text, writes at a desk, and accepts a Page, a two-monthly click and link-post reach loss in exchange. It is worth keeping in view because the draft record the capture route creates (URL, URN, title, date, text if present) is the same record a POSSE composer would write, so moving later costs the composer and the OAuth flow, not a migration.

## Your admin already holds the capture pipeline, minus one route

The site's content model is small, and that is an advantage. A LinkedIn post is a YAML file with a required `title`, `url` and `published`, optional `excerpt`, `series`, `stats`, `tags` and an `embed` flag; an article is `title` and `url` with an optional date, reading time and excerpt; neither stores body text or images ([src/lib/content-schemas.ts](../src/lib/content-schemas.ts) lines 142-166). The build refuses an `embed: true` entry whose link carries no post number, and the quality run fails a production page that still shows a `[VERIFY]` placeholder ([src/content.config.ts](../src/content.config.ts) lines 1-6). Display is finished: `linkedin-post.astro` shows title, date, series, excerpt and a "Read on LinkedIn" link, loads the official iframe only when the visitor presses "Load the post here", marks it failed after 12 seconds and keeps the card as the fallback ([src/components/embeds/linkedin-post.astro](../src/components/embeds/linkedin-post.astro); [src/components/islands/embeds.ts](../src/components/islands/embeds.ts)); articles are pure link cards because "LinkedIn does not reliably allow articles to be embedded" ([src/components/embeds/linkedin-article.astro](../src/components/embeds/linkedin-article.astro)). A capture pipeline therefore has exactly three fields to fill, and the components, states and `/_states` fixtures that CLAUDE.md section 2.6 demands already exist for them.

The write side exists too. `POST /api/resources` is the precedent: without a header it takes a visitor suggestion into a draft; with `Authorization: Bearer <RESOURCES_API_KEY>` (compared in constant time) it saves a draft under a chosen slug or, with `"publish": true`, validates with the build's own Zod schema and commits the file to `main`, answering 422 for field problems, 409 for a conflict and 503 when `GITHUB_TOKEN` is absent ([src/admin/pages/resources-api.ts](../src/admin/pages/resources-api.ts); [docs/RESOURCES_API.md](RESOURCES_API.md)). The SWOT review already says "the same pattern could take LinkedIn posts and videos" ([docs/SWOT_AND_UX_REVIEW.md](SWOT_AND_UX_REVIEW.md) line 36). `/admin/youtube` is the feed precedent: pull a public feed, dedupe against published entries and drafts, create drafts only, and "the owner opens each draft, checks it and publishes it" ([src/admin/pages/youtube.astro](../src/admin/pages/youtube.astro)). The editor's `fullLinkedinLink` already follows an `lnkd.in` short link once and strips the tracking query ([src/admin/lib/kinds.ts](../src/admin/lib/kinds.ts) lines 180-188), and `commit()` accepts several changes in one commit, so a week of drafts can go live in one Vercel build ([src/admin/lib/github.ts](../src/admin/lib/github.ts)). What is missing is narrow: a sibling route (say `POST /api/linkedin`) that tells `/pulse/` links from post links, dedupes by the 19-digit activity id against the repository and the drafts table (nothing does this today), and calls the same `saveDraft` or `publish`; a key of its own, since whether `RESOURCES_API_KEY` is set on the Vercel project cannot be read from the repository; and the channels that deliver the link to it.

The channels are where the taps live, and the tap counts below are inferences from documented mechanics rather than measurements. On Android, a small installed PWA on your own domain can register as a share target; Chrome hands the shared URL over in the `text` field, and the app must be installed first ([Chrome: Web Share Target](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/web-share-target)). Safari and iOS do not support share targets at all, the WebKit request open since 2019 ([WebKit bug 194593](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=194593)), so on an iPhone the equivalent is a Shortcut with "Show in Share Sheet" that POSTs JSON through "Get Contents of URL" with an `Authorization` header ([Apple: run a shortcut from another app](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/run-a-shortcut-from-another-app-apd163eb9f95/ios); [Apple: Get Contents of URL](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/apd58d46713f/ios)). On the desktop, LinkedIn's own pages ship a `script-src` policy of nonces, hashes and allow-listed hosts with **no `unsafe-inline` or `unsafe-eval`**, measured on 3 November 2026 ([linkedin.com headers](https://www.linkedin.com/)), which is why bookmarklets that inject a script or `eval` break there, whereas a Manifest V3 content script runs in an isolated world where the page's policy does not apply and the DOM is readable ([Chrome: Content scripts](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/content-scripts)); Edge documents loading such an extension unpacked through Developer mode ([Microsoft Learn: sideload an extension](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/extensions/getting-started/extension-sideloading)). Email-in works on every device at $0 through Cloudflare Email Workers, which hand your Worker the raw MIME with SPF and DKIM results prepended ([Cloudflare Email Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-routing/email-workers/)), or Resend's free tier with inbound included at 3,000 emails a month ([Resend pricing](https://resend.com/pricing)). One thing no source documents is what the LinkedIn app actually puts in its share payload, a URL alone or text plus URL; a single test from your phone answers it and decides whether the title arrives for free or you type the first line.

| Channel | Taps per post (inferred) | What arrives | Reads LinkedIn's page? | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Android share sheet to an installed PWA on your domain | Share, pick the app, confirm: about 3 | the post URL (the title only if the app shares text) | no | $0 |
| iPhone share sheet to a Shortcut | about 2 to 3 (+1 to paste the first line) | URL, optional pasted text | no | $0 |
| Desktop: personal unpacked extension, one toolbar click | 1 | URL, URN, first line, date from the open post | yes, in your own browser on your own click | $0 |
| Desktop: self-contained bookmarklet | 1 | the same, if it injects nothing and evals nothing | yes, the same | $0 |
| Share to Mail, to a secret inbound address | about 3 to 4 | URL and whatever you type or attach | no | $0 (Cloudflare or Resend free tiers) |
| Paste into the admin editor (today) | copy link, paste, type title, save: about 4 | anything you paste | no | $0 |

Two of these deserve a policy note. The share-sheet and email channels read nothing from LinkedIn; they carry a link you chose to share, which no clause touches. A personal extension that reads the open post on your click is the gentlest member of a category LinkedIn's help page names ("browser plug-ins, and browser extensions"), and nothing published distinguishes a one-click clipper of your own words from Shield; the exposure is small and the gain is that the title arrives without typing, so it is a choice, listed below. Whatever lands, land it as a draft: a draft in Postgres costs no build, a wrong link or a private post never reaches the public site, and publishing is the one click that already exists.

Four site facts bound the design. Vercel's Hobby plan allows **100 deployments a day**, one build at a time on a 2-vCPU machine, and "restricts users to non-commercial, personal use only" ([Vercel: Hobby plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby); [Vercel: Limits](https://vercel.com/docs/limits)); thirty publishes a month is nowhere near the cap, but the fair-use line for a job-search portfolio is yours to read. A publish to `main` keeps Vercel current and leaves the public GitHub Pages copy behind until the next `npm run sync` ([scripts/publish/sync.mjs](../scripts/publish/sync.mjs)), as every admin publish already does. The `/posts` page lists every post with no paging ([src/pages/posts.astro](../src/pages/posts.astro) line 31), so a daily habit puts 365 embed facades on one page within a year, and a limit or paging is a layout change under the design lock. And for search, the site's copy should be its own canonical: Google now says "The canonical link element is not recommended for those who want to avoid duplication by syndication partners" ([Google: Canonicalization troubleshooting](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting)), and its spam policy targets republishing "without adding any original content or value" ([Google: Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)); a title, a date, a series and a link add context rather than duplicate, while the blog's existing "originally published on LinkedIn" pattern, which points the canonical at LinkedIn ([docs/CONTENT_GUIDE.md](CONTENT_GUIDE.md) lines 161-163), hands that article's ranking to LinkedIn by design.

## Conclusion and recommendation: share once, publish once

Take the capture route. It is the only one of the three that is inside LinkedIn's terms, inside your own four rules, and buildable from parts the repository already has. Concretely: one key-protected `POST /api/linkedin` beside `/api/resources`, which normalises the link through `fullLinkedinLink`, dedupes by activity id, files `/pulse/` links as articles and the rest as posts, and saves a draft (or publishes when you send `"publish": true`); a share target on whichever phone you post from, and optionally a one-click extension on the desktop; and the admin's existing list, where the draft appears under "Waiting to be published" with its link and date filled, waiting for a title if the phone did not send one and for your Publish click. Per post that is **about three taps at the moment you post plus one click in the admin**, or three taps and nothing more if you choose straight-through publishing; on the desktop it is one click plus one. Per month it is **$0**: no service, no subscription, no token to renew, one Vercel build per publish (or per batch) on a plan that allows a hundred a day. What it cannot do: it cannot catch a post you did not share; it cannot store the post's words or pictures, because the site has no field for them, and the embed shows them only when a visitor asks; it cannot show engagement numbers, which the admin's own rule "Only a figure you can show a source for" ([src/admin/lib/kinds.ts](../src/admin/lib/kinds.ts) line 201) would keep off the card anyway; it cannot embed multi-photo posts or reposts with commentary, which fall back to the link card the component already draws; it cannot bring in the body of a LinkedIn Article, which stays a link card as today unless you republish it by hand; and it cannot back-fill, for which one "Download your data" request, collected on the desktop after the 24-to-48-hour wait, and a one-off importer of `Shares.csv` is the official path.

The alternatives are not wrong, they are different bargains. A nightly Apify or Bright Data run would remove the three taps for $0 to $1.50 a month and could fill text and images, but it makes your site a customer of a crawler LinkedIn has shown it will sue or settle out of existence, it is by definition the "outside service" your rules say to ask about first, and its data is somebody else's reading of your page rather than your own link, which sits uneasily with "never invent content" even when the words are yours. The reverse route is the one to grow into if you ever want the site to be the first home of the text: the draft record is the same, the cost is a LinkedIn Page, an OAuth flow and a 60-day click, and the long-form half of your writing stays in LinkedIn's editor regardless. The asymmetry worth remembering is LinkedIn's own: it opens the write door to everyone and keeps the read door shut, so the strongest position for a personal site is to be standing at the publish moment, not polling afterwards. The draft inbox you would build for this is also general: YouTube already feeds it, and if `r_member_social` ever reopens, an API poll would land in the same place.

| | Capture at publish (recommended) | Nightly scraper | Publish from the site |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taps per post | about 3 on the phone + 1 to publish (1 + 1 on the desktop) | 0 (+1 to publish a draft) | writing moves into the admin; publish is 1 click |
| Money per month | $0 | $0 to $1.50 (Apify credit or Bright Data free records) | $0 with your own app, or $0 to $5.99 hosted (Zernio, Publora) |
| Inside LinkedIn's User Agreement | yes | no (the vendor is in breach; your account is not exposed on cookie-free actors) | yes |
| New outside service | none | Apify or Bright Data | a LinkedIn developer app and a Page, or a hosted partner |
| Recurring chore | none | watch for breakage | re-consent every 60 days; version bump yearly |
| Gets post text and images | no (unless the schema grows) | yes | yes |
| Covers LinkedIn Articles | as link cards | some actors claim article scraping | no API authors a Pulse article |
| Covers posts written in the LinkedIn app | only those you share | yes | no |

## Decisions only you can make

1. Approve the capture route as a change to the content system: a new inbound API route, a share target or Shortcut on your phone, and whether a personal desktop extension (which reads your own open post in your own browser) is acceptable, or the share-sheet channels alone are enough.
2. Draft-first or straight-through: whether a captured post waits for your Publish click (recommended) or goes live on capture with `"publish": true`.
3. Whether the site should ever store a post's body and images, which is a schema change and a new display state, or stay with title, link, date and the click-to-load embed.
4. Whether a cookie-free scraper is acceptable at all, knowing it sits outside LinkedIn's User Agreement and can disappear as Proxycurl did; if yes, Apify harvestapi's 24-hour window feeding drafts is the shape.
5. Whether to move your writing desk into the admin (publish-from-site), which means creating a LinkedIn Page for the developer app and re-consenting every 60 days, or to keep composing in the LinkedIn app.
6. A limit or paging rule for `/posts` before a daily habit makes it a 365-card page (a layout change under the design lock).
7. Wording on the embed facade that tells visitors LinkedIn sets its own cookies when a post is loaded (an enhancement; the measurement shows one-year `bcookie` and `bscookie` on load).
8. Whether a job-search portfolio counts as non-commercial under Vercel's Hobby terms, or whether to move to Pro at $20 a seat.
9. Whether republished LinkedIn Articles keep pointing their canonical at LinkedIn, as the blog pattern does today, or become self-canonical so that the site rather than LinkedIn ranks.
10. One "Download your data" request to back-fill older posts, and a one-time test of what your phone's LinkedIn app shares (URL only, or text and URL), which decides whether the title arrives by itself; plus one press of "Load the post here" on `/posts` to confirm the activity-form embeds the site builds actually load, since only a share URN was measured.
